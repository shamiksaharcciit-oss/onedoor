## 1. Q1 — it is a derivation record, and the constitution changes to say so

Delivery's lean is approved, and the flagged contradiction is resolved by **amending
principle 5, not by stretching it**. The word "receipt" was coined when every derivation
in this product was recomputable; the word carried that promise implicitly because
nothing yet existed that couldn't keep it. S6 is the first thing that can't. The
programme already owns the law that decides this — *a ruling's own vocabulary is not
exempt from the ruling's law* — and a constitution that lets its own noun outrun the
computation is R050 §4's defect installed at the top of the document.

**Principle 5's amended wording, verbatim:**

> Every derivation gets a record. A record that promises re-derivation is a receipt; a
> record that cannot promise it says so on its face. No derivation goes unrecorded, and
> no record claims more than its computation delivers.

The amendment is recorded in the constitution's own change history with this memo cited.
Every existing receipt already satisfies the new wording; nothing else moves.

The **derivation record** carries what §7.1 proposes: its own schema, the instrument
(model id and version, prompt digest, `PACK_DIGEST`, sampling parameters), the frozen
description's digest, the resulting `policy_digest`, and the face statement in
`PROJECTION_NOTE`'s shape — in substance: *this record attests what was produced, from
what, by what instrument; it does not attest that the same inputs would produce it
again.* One more sentence belongs on that face: **the candidate's authority comes from
the checks it passes, never from this record** — a derivation record confers provenance,
not trust. Provenance and re-derivability are different claims, and the vocabulary now
keeps them apart. (Note for the wider programme: onetrace's stage receipts will meet
this same boundary at every model-backed stage; the split ruled here is the split there.)

## 2. Q2 — the mechanism is approved; the field is `proposer_provenance: live | fixture`

Injectable interface, deterministic fixture proposer for CI, real client behind an
extra, hard-at-point-of-use refusal naming the remedy — approved as proposed. The field
takes the **same value pair as `ledger_provenance`**: the label's job is identical — was
this the real instrument or the shipped stand-in — and a renderer that already speaks
`live | fixture` must not learn a second dialect for the same distinction. "Which model"
is the instrument block's job, not the label's. Two requirements travel with it: the
instrument block is **never empty in either case** — a fixture records its own identity,
version and digest exactly as a model records its id — and the label survives to every
rendering with B5's test shape: a sabotage that strips it fails exactly the labelling
tests.

## 3. Q3 — adjacent, not merged; one surface, two sections, never one table

Delivery's lean is approved, and the principle-4 counterargument is answered head-on:
merging would not produce one honest list, it would produce one dishonest list, because
**a list is honest only if every row carries the same kind of warrant**. `declared_inert`
is a fact about the engine; *mentioned-but-unruled* is a model's reading of a sentence.
The two-places-to-look cost is real and is paid with rendering, not with schema: **one
surface, two sections** — the coverage map's measured rows, then the proposal's
mentioned-but-unruled rows — with the second section stating its warrant on its face
(*a model's reading of the description, not a measurement*) and each row citing the
coverage state it was checked against, map digest included. One page to look at; two
kinds of row that never share a table.

## 4. Q4 — the demo gate is disclosure-shaped, not score-shaped

Delivery asked what threshold gates the demo. **None does — and that is a harder gate,
not a softer one.** A threshold we choose for our own generator is the instrument fitted
to the finding, one step earlier — the same shape the canary channel refused. The rule:
**the demo may run when the benchmark's results, misses included, are published beside
it, and the demo states its number.** A generator demonstrated with its misses on the
table is honest at any score; demonstrated without them, dishonest at any score. The
corpus must include the adversarial descriptions §9 names — text crafted to talk the
model into permissive rules — and the published misses must include the security-shaped
ones, not only the quality-shaped ones. Benchmark runs against a live model are
Shamik-credentialed, budgeted, and scheduled outside CI; CI benchmarks the fixture path
only. R036 stands: a bad number delays a demo, never the release.
