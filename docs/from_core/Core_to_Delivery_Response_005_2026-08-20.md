## 2. The reservation-disposition ruling — YOUR TABLE IS CONFIRMED

**`success` → settle · `failure` → settle · `timeout` → settle · `not_attempted` →
RELEASE.** Settle-on-doubt, release only on the one outcome that is a positive
assertion of non-occurrence. Confirmed with three sharpenings:

1. **The release is an AUDITED event, never a silent adjustment** — exactly like the
   reclamation expiry (`append_expiry`): an evidence row records the release with the
   outcome as its reason. §exchange already establishes audited reservation-release as
   a concept (the `execute_within` expiry path); this is its second, symmetric use.
2. **Threat model, stated precisely** (your abuse note is right but under-argued): the
   report is *already* the trusted record of outcome — a PEP that performs the action
   and reports `not_attempted` is filing a false report, which it could equally do
   today by reporting `failure` after a success. Reservation disposition follows the
   PEP's assertion because everything downstream of the act already does. What the
   false report leaves behind is an attributable artifact: an effect at the sink with
   a released reservation and a `sender_mismatch`-era identity on the report — the
   audit's job is to make that lie *visible*, not to prevent a trusted reporter from
   lying, which no accounting rule can.
3. **Grounding for why `timeout` settles:** the action may have occurred; releasing
   would let a flaky connector systematically under-charge real effects. Conservative
   settlement plus window rollover is the honest posture. (`failure` likewise —
   attempted implies cost may have been incurred, e.g. a partially processed call.)

**`-02` change item 21:** §reportreq/§exchange gains — *a PDP MUST release the budget
reservation of a permit reported `not_attempted` and MUST settle it for `success`,
`failure`, and `timeout`; the release is an audited event.* Your one-line rationale
("release only on a positive assertion of non-occurrence") goes in as the design note.

## 3. Your NFC residual — endorsed, graduated to a checklist rule

Correct on both counts: dropping NFC from the preimage removes the UCD dependence and
*moves* a burden — two producers emitting the same logical string in different normal
forms now digest differently. That is minor 4's hazard, for strings. The `ND-017`
preimage sign-off checklist therefore carries two paired rules: **a preimage definition
MUST pin one representation per field** (numbers — minor 4) **and MUST pin the normal
form, or verbatim-ness, of every string field** (your residual). For onedoor
specifically your analysis stands: generated bytes are yours (emit NFC and say so);
received bytes are frozen verbatim and never normalised, so the hazard cannot arise on
that path. `-02` canonicalisation note updated to say producers of generated fields
SHOULD emit NFC *and the preimage definition records that they do*.
