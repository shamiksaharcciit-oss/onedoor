## 1. Q1 — `cost_eur`: proposal sustained, with one law attached

**Derive from `policy.cost_param` where the candidate policy declares one; declare the
gap where it does not.** Sustained as proposed — it is faithful by the same mechanism
the live engine uses, which makes the *instrument* identical rather than merely the
answer plausible. Defaulting to zero is rejected for the reason delivery already named:
a default that looks like a fact, understating cap denials in the direction of
reassurance — the worst direction.

The attached law, which the receipt must honor structurally: **measured zero and
declared zero never share a representation.** A cost of `0.00` computed from
`params_json` via `cost_param` is a measurement and participates in cap accounting. An
action whose policy declares no `cost_param` is not a zero — it is a non-measurement,
counted in `coverage.skipped` under its own named reason (`cost_underivable` or
delivery's better name), and cap-driven divergence for those actions is stated as
uncovered on the face of the receipt. A test should assert the two cases produce
different receipts.

One boundary made explicit since replay evaluates the *candidate*: the candidate's
`cost_param` is the one that applies, even where it differs from the policy that was
active when the row was sealed. That is not a bug; the backtest's question is "what
would the candidate have done," and the candidate includes its own cost declaration.

## 2. Q2 — no third value, and one refusal delivery didn't ask for

**Two labels stand.** The proposal is sustained: `ledger_provenance` describes the
**cited range**, not the store. A mixed store — sealed chain with an unchained prefix —
is `live` over the chained span, with the prefix counted in `coverage.skipped` under a
named reason (`unchained_prefix`), because *a range that cannot be cited is not
replayed*. Two labels plus a counted skip beats three labels; a label that needs a
footnote to be told apart from its neighbor is a footnote wearing a label's clothes.

The case the question implies but does not ask: **a store with no chain at all** —
chaining is opt-in and off by default, so this is common, not exotic. Ruled: the
backtest engine **refuses to run**, with a stated reason and the remedy in the message
(enable chaining; rows sealed from today are citable tomorrow). `row_hash_at_last_seq`
is REQUIRED in the receipt precisely so this door cannot be left open — a receipt whose
citation is null is the store vouching for itself, which this product never does. The
refusal is a fail-closed dial and, not incidentally, the Studio teaching the deployer
why the chain exists. The fixture ledger is the honest demo path for that deployment in
the meantime.

## 3. Q3 — in the wheel, byte-pinned, and the pinning earns its weight twice

**In the package**, under `onedoor/studio/_fixture/`, generated by a script whose output
is deterministic (timestamps pinned as inputs, not sampled) and **byte-pinned** with a
CI test asserting regeneration reproduces the committed bytes — the `docs/oneview/`
discipline, as proposed. Size discipline: keep it in the low hundreds of rows and state
the byte count in the ticket; if it cannot stay under ~256 KB, that is worth a line back
to this board before packaging, not after.

And name the property the pinning buys beyond reproducibility: **the fixture's chain
head is a published constant.** Every install ships the same fixture bytes, so its
`row_hash` values are public. A fixture-backed receipt stripped of its label and
presented as `live` is therefore *checkable by anyone* — compare the cited
`row_hash_at_last_seq` against the shipped fixture's known head. B5's label-survival
tests guard the renderings; the pinning guards the world outside them. Put one test on
it: a receipt citing a known fixture hash while claiming `live` is detected by the
verifier and named for what it is.
