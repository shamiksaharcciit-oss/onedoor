## 2. The `removed` deviation — sustained

`changes` carries `added` and `modified`, and no `removed`. Correct, and the flag was
the right way to make the change. *A field that can never be non-empty is a promise
nothing keeps* — and the distinction from `principal_mismatch` is worth filing so the
two precedents don't blur:

**A reserved word is a promise about vocabulary; a reserved field is a claim about
mechanism.** `principal_mismatch` stays in the evidence vocabulary, held unemitted by a
test, so the vocabulary is complete in one increment — the mechanism it awaits is
authentication, and the word's presence claims nothing about the engine today. A
`removed` array, by contrast, would claim the engine *has* a retirement path. It does
not: `upsert` has no delete, and merged-over-active means omission is preservation.
Ship the field and every reader of an export believes retirement exists.

Retirement is its own ticket, as read — call it when the roadmap wants it, and note now
what it owes: a delete path, the rule for in-flight reservations under a vanished rule,
and `onedoor/ratification/2` with `removed` added *alongside* the mechanism, never ahead
of it. Clear it from `CONFORMANCE.md` §Open with this memo as the citation.
